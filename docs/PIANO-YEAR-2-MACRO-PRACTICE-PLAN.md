# Piano Year 2 — Macro Practice Plan

**Planning horizon:** 17 August 2026–15 August 2027  
**Purpose:** sequence the repertoire, technical development and Harmony Lab work before designing piece-specific micro plans.

## 1. Starting profile

The strongest signal in the first-year report is not simply that several pieces are memorised. It is the speed with which new contrapuntal material is becoming organised: BWV 926 was acquired in about a week and BWV 772 in two learning sequences over less than two weeks, with improvement from session to session. This suggests a genuine strength in pattern recognition, two-voice thinking and memory consolidation.

The main developmental bottleneck is now the conversion of fast acquisition into mature performance:

- relaxed fluency at the intended tempo;
- reliable voice hierarchy rather than merely correct notes;
- ease in arpeggiated and descending figures;
- tone and balance on a weighted action;
- long-form continuity, recovery and refinement after memorisation.

The year should therefore preserve the Bach/counterpoint advantage while preventing an expanding backlog of memorised-but-unfinished pieces.

## 2. Planning assumptions

- Available piano-practice capacity is **two hours every day (about 14 hours per week)**, with theory study on weekends.
- Three repertoire pieces may be active at once, but only **one is the primary acquisition/memorisation project**.
- The second slot is structural: score-based learning, counterpoint, continuity or consolidation.
- The third slot is interpretive or corrective: voicing, touch, pedal, tempo, tension or a previously learned piece.
- Short maintenance runs do not count as a fourth active project. They are deliberately brief and rotated.
- The Debussy Arabesque is confirmed as **Arabesque No. 1**.
- The two Chopin nocturnes are confirmed as **Op. 37 No. 1 in G minor**, followed by **Op. 9 No. 2 in E-flat major**.
- The complete Motovilov arrangement of the Passacaglia is the project target.
- The 88-key weighted instrument is expected in **one to two months (approximately weeks 4–8)**. Weighted-action repair begins when it arrives; the calendar does not force it earlier.

### Weekly capacity and load control

Two hours every day makes the repertoire breadth feasible, but seven daily sessions should not become seven high-intensity sessions. Daily contact is retained while one day is deliberately lower-load: slow playing, score study, listening, internal starts, theory and recording review rather than repeated full-force runs.

The following is the macro allocation of the 14 weekly piano hours. The micro plan will distribute it across individual days and measures.

| Practice function | Approximate weekly time | Daily-average equivalent |
|---|---:|---:|
| Slot A — primary acquisition/memory | 5 h 50 min | 50 min |
| Slot B — structural work | 3 h 30 min | 30 min |
| Slot C — interpretation/repair | 2 h 20 min | 20 min |
| Harmony Lab / technique | 1 h 10 min | 10 min |
| Maintenance | 1 h 10 min | 10 min |
| Recording, listening and review | 1 h 10 min | 10 min |
| **Total** | **14 h** | **120 min/day** |

Weekend theory is treated as supplementary. If it is actually included inside the two hours, it should replace part of the weekend maintenance/review allocation rather than removing the primary piece's continuity.

## 3. What “learned this year” means

Repertoire is assigned to three outcome levels so that breadth does not masquerade as mastery.

| Outcome | Meaning | Planned repertoire |
|---|---|---|
| **Performance-ready** | Secure score/memory, relaxed continuity, intended voice hierarchy, musically credible tempo, and at least one satisfactory recording | BWV 772; complete Motovilov Passacaglia; complete BWV 528; BWV 927; BWV 855 Prelude and Fugue; both Chopin nocturnes; ideally Debussy Arabesque No. 1 |
| **Fluent study-complete** | Notes and structure secure, playable end to end, but tone, tempo, memory or interpretation still developing | Clair de lune; Arabesque No. 1 if it needs further polishing; any performance-ready target that needs more time |
| **Substantial stretch project** | Entire score surveyed and sectioned; a substantial connected portion learned correctly; whole-work completion pursued only while quality gates remain satisfied | Chopin, Andante spianato in G major, Op. 22, without the Grande Polonaise |

This is intentionally ambitious. Fourteen weekly piano hours make both nocturnes and Arabesque No. 1 reasonable performance targets. They do not remove the technical gap between the present repertoire and the Andante: making both Debussy works and the complete Andante fully memorised and performance-ready in the same year remains conditional on the earlier quality gates, not on available hours alone.

## 4. The three-slot operating system

| Slot | Share of repertoire time | Function | Admission rule |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **A — Primary** | 45–50% | New material and deliberate memorisation | Exactly one piece; normally a six- to ten-week residency |
| **B — Structural** | 25–30% | Score reading, hands/voices, continuity, harmonic map, or consolidation | May be new only at low intensity; no competing memorisation campaign |
| **C — Interpretive/repair** | 15–20% | Tone, voicing, pedal, tension, tempo or a known problem passage | One sharply defined outcome, not a vague “play it more” assignment |
| **Maintenance** | 5–10% | Keep established repertoire retrievable | One short cold start or run; rotate pieces rather than play everything every day |

When Slot A graduates, Slot B may be promoted. A new piece enters Slot B only after another piece leaves the active set.

### Fast-track rule

If two consecutive Slot-A projects pass all graduation tests at least two weeks early, bank the saved time rather than adding an unrelated piece. Use it in this order:

1. promote Clair de lune from study-complete to performance-ready;
2. extend the Andante toward complete study and performance;
3. deepen existing recordings and performance reliability.

## 6. Composer and style architecture

### Bach as the year-long backbone

BWV 772, 528, 927 and 855 form one continuous line: two-voice clarity → sustained multi-voice continuity → compact prelude fluency → WTC-scale structural and contrapuntal control. Bach remains present in every block, but not always as the memorisation project.

### Chopin as the voice-leading and tone strand

The two existing short preludes already show that singing lines over supporting harmony are developing well. The Raindrop Prelude exposes the remaining balance problem. A relatively contained nocturne should therefore precede Debussy and the Andante; a second nocturne then tests whether the skill transfers rather than belonging to only one piece.

### Debussy after weighted-action acclimatisation

Arabesque No. 1 is placed before Clair de lune to introduce continuous figurations, layered sound and flexible pulse before the more exposed colour, pedal and long melodic projection of Clair de lune. Both begin score-based; memorisation is optional until the sound and fingering are stable.

### Andante spianato as a stretch capstone

The Andante is not excluded. It is protected from premature full-scale memorisation. Its promotion depends on successful nocturne voicing, Debussy tone control, relaxed arpeggio work on the weighted action, and an empty Slot A.

## 7. Graduation and readiness gates

A piece does not graduate merely because the notes are memorised. It needs:

1. two cold starts on different days without restarting after a local error;
2. an end-to-end run at a musically credible tempo with no increasing tension;
3. a stated voice plan, with the important voice playable alone where relevant;
4. recovery from at least three deliberately chosen internal starting points;
5. one recording judged for pulse, balance, tension and phrase direction;
6. a successful run on the weighted 88-key instrument when touch or register is material.

### Andante promotion gate

Full acquisition of the Andante may begin only when all are true:

- one Chopin nocturne is performance-ready and the second is at least fluent;
- one Debussy piece is at least study-complete;
- tonic arpeggios and the K. 545 descending figure are relaxed on the weighted action;
- ornaments can be inserted without stiffening the melodic hand;
- Slot A is free and no earlier piece has an unresolved tension problem.

## 8. Existing-repertoire maintenance and repair

Do not run the entire first-year repertoire every week. Use four rotating pools:

| Pool | Pieces | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Stable performance** | BWV 934, BWV 999, Chopin Op. 28 Nos. 4 and 6 | One piece per week, cold start or complete run |
| **Recent consolidation** | BWV 926, BWV 772, Passacaglia, later new graduates | Each graduate weekly for four weeks, then fortnightly |
| **Targeted repair** | C-major Prelude, BWV 935, K. 545, Raindrop Prelude | Only one or two at a time; each receives a written defect and exit test |
| **Archive** | Any piece not currently serving a skill goal | One retrieval run every four to six weeks |

The repair wording should remain specific: “descending arpeggio remains loose at X tempo” or “melody remains audible over the accompaniment in measures Y–Z,” never simply “needs more practice.”

## 9. Harmony Lab’s macro role

The current `fable_refactor` branch contains a 405-leaf harmony curriculum, MIDI validation, target playback, curriculum progress, an interleaved review queue, and 25 live Piano Technique leaves (one five-finger warm-up plus 24 two-octave tonic-arpeggio runs). It should support the repertoire plan, not compete with it.

| Annual blocks | Lab curriculum emphasis | Repertoire purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Inversions, cadences, motives, polyphonic harmony; Piano Technique warm-up/arpeggio subset | Passacaglia bass architecture, Bach voice ownership, weighted-action transition |
| 3–4 | Voice-leading cadences, bass-line dictation, harmonic minor, seventh chords, polyphonic harmony | BWV 528 continuity, BWV 855 chromatic/contrapuntal hearing, Nocturne bass-to-melody hierarchy |
| 5–6 | Seventh chords, melodic minor, applied chords, selected cadence reviews | Chopin colour and tendency tones; Debussy sonority without forcing every chord into a functional label |
| 7 | Review queue across previously admitted categories; section-level analysis | Retention, Clair de lune colour, Andante prerequisite audit |

Macro limits for the Lab:

- use only a piece-relevant subset of the 405 leaves;
- admit no more than one or two new Lab concepts in a repertoire block;
- let the review queue interleave only material already admitted by the annual plan;
- keep Lab work to roughly 10–15% of total practice until the micro plan specifies otherwise;
- use its grading for pitches, chord coverage, ordered notes and bass position, but not as proof of good tone, rhythm, dynamics, pedal or physical technique.

## 10. Branch-specific preparation for the later micro plan

- Curated Score Soul harmonic maps currently exist only for **BWV 846** and **BWV 999**. These are ideal calibration/maintenance pieces.
- Importable score resources already include the present repertoire, Passacaglia, BWV 528, BWV 772 and a **BWV 855a prelude** file.
- The repository does not currently provide the complete BWV 855 Prelude and Fugue package, BWV 927, the proposed nocturnes, the Debussy pieces or the Andante spianato as ready repertoire resources.
- The later micro-plan phase should therefore begin with a score/resource inventory and should distinguish three routes: existing curated map, importable score without curated analysis, and missing score requiring import.
- Harmony Lab is not yet an annual repertoire scheduler. For now, the block table above is the source of truth; the Lab dashboard tracks drill mastery and due reviews.

## 11. Review cadence

- **Weekly:** check whether Slots A/B/C still have distinct jobs; remove redundant work.
- **Every four weeks:** make one cold recording and compare it against the piece’s single current defect.
- **At each block boundary:** graduate, extend or retain the primary piece using the six tests above.
- **At weeks 26 and 46:** rebalance the year. If quality is behind, defer the Andante before sacrificing the Bach/Nocturne/Debussy foundations.

## 12. Confirmed parameters and micro-plan entry point

- Piano practice: two hours daily, seven days per week.
- Theory study: weekends.
- Weighted 88-key keyboard: expected in one to two months; MIDI capability still to be confirmed when the model is chosen.
- Debussy: Arabesque No. 1.
- Chopin nocturnes: Op. 37 No. 1, followed by Op. 9 No. 2.
- Passacaglia: complete Motovilov arrangement.

The first micro plan should cover **Block 1 only**: Passacaglia as the primary acquisition project, BWV 772 as the fluency/voice-leading graduation project, and the first half of BWV 528 as the structural continuity project. It should translate the two-hour daily allocation into measures, memory units, Lab exercises, tempo/tension checkpoints, recordings and exit evidence.
