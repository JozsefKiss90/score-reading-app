# Harmony Lab Symbol Decoder

## Beginner Companion to BWV 846’s Full Measure Map

**Companion to:** `HARMONY-LAB-PIECE-PRACTICE-MANUAL-UC01-BWV846.md`  
**Purpose:** turn chord symbols and Roman numerals into actual notes and simple harmonic meanings  
**Level:** basic → early intermediate  
**Edition basis:** the app’s 34-measure BWV 846 map  

---

## 1. The four layers kept separate

The full measure map combines four kinds of information. Read them separately at first.

| Layer | Question | Example | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Key/scale** | What is “home,” and which notes normally belong? | C major | Home is C; normal notes are C–D–E–F–G–A–B |
| **Chord symbol** | Which actual notes form the chord? | F♯°7 | F♯–A–C–E♭ |
| **Roman numeral** | Where does the chord come from in this key? | vii°7/V | Leading-tone diminished seventh of the V chord |
| **Function** | What job does it perform? | temporary dominant | It creates tension directed toward G |

### One chord, four descriptions

| Layer | Measure 22 example |
|---|---|
| Key | C major |
| Actual chord | F♯°7 = F♯–A–C–E♭ |
| Roman label | vii°7/V |
| Function/destination | A leading-tone chord pointing to G, which is V in C major |

The Roman numeral is therefore not a second chord. It is an explanation of the first chord.

## 2. Key, scale and chord are not the same thing

| Term | Simple definition | BWV 846 example |
|---|---|---|
| **Key** | The musical home and the hierarchy around it | C major; C feels like home |
| **Scale** | The ordered note collection belonging to the key | C–D–E–F–G–A–B–C |
| **Scale degree** | A note’s numbered place in the scale | C=1, D=2, E=3, F=4, G=5, A=6, B=7 |
| **Chord** | Selected notes sounding as one harmonic unit | D–F–A–C = Dm7 |
| **Progression** | Several chords heard in order | Dm7 → G7 → C |

## 3. The C-major master table

Build chords by taking every other scale note. For a triad take three notes; for a seventh chord take four.

| Degree | Scale note/root | Triad notes | Triad symbol | Roman | Seventh notes | Seventh symbol | Usual simple role |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C | C–E–G | C | I | C–E–G–B | Cmaj7 | home |
| 2 | D | D–F–A | Dm | ii | D–F–A–C | Dm7 | prepares tension |
| 3 | E | E–G–B | Em | iii | E–G–B–D | Em7 | colour/bridge |
| 4 | F | F–A–C | F | IV | F–A–C–E | Fmaj7 | moves away/prepares |
| 5 | G | G–B–D | G | V | G–B–D–F | G7 | tension pointing home |
| 6 | A | A–C–E | Am | vi | A–C–E–G | Am7 | home-family colour |
| 7 | B | B–D–F | B° | vii° | B–D–F–A | Bø7 | leading-tone tension |

### Roman-numeral case and symbols

| Mark | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Uppercase numeral | Major triad | V = G major |
| Lowercase numeral | Minor triad | ii = D minor |
| `°` | Diminished chord | vii° = B–D–F |
| `7` | Add a seventh above the triad | V7 = G–B–D–F |
| `maj7` in a chord symbol | Major triad plus major seventh | Cmaj7 = C–E–G–B |
| `ø7` | Half-diminished seventh | Bø7 = B–D–F–A |
| `°7` | Fully diminished seventh | B°7 = B–D–F–A♭ |

Important distinction: the naturally occurring seventh chord on degree 7 of C major is **Bø7**, containing A. BWV 846 also uses **B°7**, containing A♭. That A♭ makes it chromatic and increases its pull toward C.

## 4. Chord-symbol decoder

Read a chord symbol from left to right.

| Part | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Root letter | What note is the chord built on? | `D` in Dm7 |
| Accidental | Is the root raised or lowered? | `F♯` in F♯°7 |
| Quality/suffix | Is it major, minor, diminished, seventh, suspended…? | `m7`, `°7`, `7`, `maj7` |
| Slash note | Which note must be lowest? | `/B` in G7/B |

### Common chord-symbol suffixes in this piece

| Symbol pattern | Meaning | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Letter only | Major triad | C | C–E–G |
| `m` | Minor triad | Am | A–C–E |
| `°` or `dim` | Diminished triad | B° | B–D–F |
| `maj7` | Major seventh chord | Cmaj7 | C–E–G–B |
| `m7` | Minor seventh chord | Dm7 | D–F–A–C |
| `7` | Dominant-type seventh chord | G7 | G–B–D–F |
| `°7` or `dim7` | Fully diminished seventh | F♯°7 | F♯–A–C–E♭ |
| `sus` | A normal chord tone is delayed/replaced by a suspended note | G11sus | G–C–D–F in the app’s map |
| `(13)` | Core seventh chord with upper colour tones | G7(13) | Core G–B–D–F, with added colour including E |
| `/letter` | Put that letter in the bass | G7/B | G7 notes, with B lowest |

## 5. Two different meanings of a slash

This is the most important notation distinction in the handout.

| Notation type | Example | Read aloud | What the slash means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chord symbol** | G7/B | “G seven over B” | B is the bass note |
| **Roman numeral** | V7/V | “five-seven of five” | The chord is the dominant of the V chord |
| **Roman numeral** | vii°7/ii | “diminished seven of two” | The chord is the leading-tone seventh of ii |
| **Roman numeral figure** | V6/5 | “five six-five” | First inversion of a V7 chord; its third is in the bass |

### Quick test

| Symbol | Is the slash followed by a letter or Roman numeral? | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| G7/B | Letter B | Bass instruction |
| C/G | Letter G | Bass instruction |
| V7/V | Roman V | Temporary harmonic target |
| V7/IV | Roman IV | Temporary harmonic target |
| vii°7/V | Roman V | Temporary harmonic target |
| vii°7/ii | Roman ii | Temporary harmonic target |

## 6. Inversion numbers without the jargon overload

An inversion changes the lowest note, not the identity of the chord.

### Triads

| Roman figure | Bass position | C-major example | Actual chord symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| I or I5/3 | Root in bass | C–E–G | C |
| I6 | Third in bass | E–G–C | C/E |
| I6/4 | Fifth in bass | G–C–E | C/G |

### Seventh chords

| Roman figure | Bass chord member | G7 example | Actual chord symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| V7 | Root G | G–B–D–F | G7 |
| V6/5 | Third B | B–D–F–G | G7/B |
| V4/3 | Fifth D | D–F–G–B | G7/D |
| V4/2 | Seventh F | F–G–B–D | G7/F |

For interpreting BWV 846, you do not need to calculate the intervals represented by every number immediately. First associate the label with the required bass position.

## 7. Function in plain language

| Function family | Plain meaning | Common C-major members | Expected feeling/direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tonic** | Home or home-family colour | I, vi; sometimes iii | Rest, stability or return |
| **Predominant** | Moves away and prepares tension | ii, ii7, IV, IV7 | Forward motion toward V |
| **Dominant** | Tension pointing toward home | V, V7, vii°, vii°7 | Wants to resolve to I |
| **Applied/temporary dominant** | Tension pointing to a chord other than I | V7/V, V7/IV, vii°7/V, vii°7/ii | Makes the target briefly feel like a local home |

### The “of” rule

For `X/Y`, read the symbol on the **right first**.

| Symbol | Step 1: target on right | Step 2: build the chord on left | Actual result in C major |
|---|---|---|---|
| V7/V | V of C is G | Dominant seventh of G is D7 | D–F♯–A–C |
| V7/IV | IV of C is F | Dominant seventh of F is C7 | C–E–G–B♭ |
| vii°7/V | V of C is G | Leading-tone diminished seventh of G is F♯°7 | F♯–A–C–E♭ |
| vii°7/ii | ii of C is Dm | Leading-tone diminished seventh of D is C♯°7 | C♯–E–G–B♭ |

## 8. Why F♯°7 and vii°7/V are the same chord

| Label | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **F♯°7** | Actual root and notes: F♯–A–C–E♭ |
| **vii°7/V** | Context: F♯ is the leading note below G, and G is V in C major |
| **temporary dominant function** | Job: create strong tension toward G |

The three labels are comparable to describing a person by name, job and destination. They do not name three different people.

## 9. Progressions translated into actual chords

The long dash `–` or arrow `→` simply means “then.”

### ii7–V7–I in C major

| Step | Roman | Actual chord | Notes | Simple role |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ii7 | Dm7 | D–F–A–C | prepare |
| 2 | V7 | G7 | G–B–D–F | tension |
| 3 | I | C | C–E–G | home |

```text
Dm7 → G7 → C
prepare → tension → home
```

### Useful note motion inside ii7–V7–I

| From | To | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Dm7 → G7 | D and F can remain; A can move to G; C can move to B | Two common tones plus two small movements |
| G7 → C | B rises to C; F falls to E | The characteristic dominant resolution |

### Opening of BWV 846: I–ii7–V6/5–I

| Measure | Roman | Actual chord | Notes | Bass clue |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I | C | C–E–G | C/home |
| 2 | ii7 | Dm7 | D–F–A–C | The app describes tonic support beneath the sonority |
| 3 | V6/5 | G7/B | G–B–D–F | B is lowest and tends upward to C |
| 4 | I | C | C–E–G | C/home returns |

### Applied examples

| Roman progression | Actual chords | Plain translation | BWV 846 measures |
|---|---|---|---:|
| V7/V → V | D7 → G | Temporary dominant of G resolves to G | 6–7, 10–11 |
| V7/IV → IV | C7 → F | Temporary dominant of F resolves to F | 20–21 |
| vii°7/ii → ii6 | C♯°7 → Dm/F | Leading-tone chord of D resolves to D minor in first inversion | 12–13 |
| vii°7/V → dominant field | F♯°7 → G-based harmony | Leading-tone chord of G intensifies the dominant | 22 and 27 |
| vii°7 → I6 | B°7 → C/E | Leading-tone chord of C resolves to C in first inversion | 14–15 |

## 10. Same root, different chord or job

One altered note can completely change the meaning.

### Chords rooted on C

| Symbol | Notes | Roman/job in C major | Recognition clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | C–E–G | I, home | Plain tonic triad |
| Cmaj7 | C–E–G–B | Imaj7, home with colour | B natural adds a gentle seventh |
| C7 | C–E–G–B♭ | V7/IV, points to F | B becomes B♭ |
| C/E | E–G–C | I6, tonic with E bass | Same C chord, different bass |
| C/G | G–C–E | I6/4; in mm.25/28 it supports dominant tension | Same C notes over G bass |

### Chords rooted on D

| Symbol | Notes | Roman/job in C major | Recognition clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dm | D–F–A | ii, predominant | F natural |
| Dm7 | D–F–A–C | ii7, predominant | F natural plus C |
| D7 | D–F♯–A–C | V7/V, points to G | F changes to F♯ |

### Chords rooted on G

| Symbol | Notes | Roman/job in C major | Recognition clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| G | G–B–D | V, dominant | Basic tension triad |
| G/B | B–D–G | V6 | Same G chord with B bass |
| G7 | G–B–D–F | V7 | F creates the seventh and stronger tension |
| G7/B | B–D–F–G | V6/5 | G7 with leading-tone B in the bass |
| G11sus | G–C–D–F | Dominant suspension | B is delayed/absent; C and F keep tension unresolved |

### Degree-seven chords

| Symbol | Notes | Status | Destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| B° | B–D–F | Diatonic diminished triad, vii° | C |

## 13. A repeatable symbol-to-notes method

Use the following as a reference, not as a timed drill.

| Step | Action | Example: vii°7/V in C major |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | State the home key | C major |
| 2 | Read the target to the right of `/` | V = G |
| 3 | Find the note one semitone below the target | F♯ is below G |
| 4 | Build the diminished seventh upward | F♯–A–C–E♭ |
| 5 | Give the actual chord symbol | F♯°7 |
| 6 | State the function in plain language | Temporary leading-tone tension pointing to G |
| 7 | Find it in the score | Measures 22 and 27 |

### Reverse direction: notes to meaning

| Given | Question | Example answer |
|---|---|---|
| F♯–A–C–E♭ | What is the root/shape? | F♯°7 |
| F♯°7 in C major | Which ordinary C-major chord contains G as its root? | V = G |
| F♯ is a semitone below G | What might the chord be doing? | Acting as vii°7/V and pointing to G |

## 15. How to use this companion with the main table

When a row in section 6.6 looks cryptic:

1. Read the **actual chord symbol** first and locate its notes in sections 4, 10 or 11.
2. Decode the **Roman numeral** using sections 3, 5–7 and 14.
3. State the **function** in one word: home, prepare, tension, or temporary tension.
4. Identify the **destination** from the next row.
5. Play the actual chord as a simple close-position block before restoring Bach’s arpeggio.

### Minimal annotation to add beside a measure

Instead of writing only `vii°7/V`, write:

```text
F♯°7 = F♯–A–C–E♭ → points to G
```

Keep the Roman numeral as a second label until the association becomes automatic.

## 17. Terms used in this supplement

| Term | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| **Root** | The note the chord is named after |
| **Bass** | The lowest sounding note |
| **Quality** | Major, minor, diminished, seventh, suspended, etc. |
| **Inversion** | Same chord identity with a different chord member in the bass |
| **Diatonic** | Made only from the normal notes of the current scale |
| **Chromatic** | Uses at least one note outside that scale |
| **Leading tone** | A note one semitone below its target, strongly pointing upward |
| **Target** | The chord toward which an applied chord resolves |
| **Applied chord** | Temporary tension borrowed to point at a non-tonic chord |
| **Function** | The chord’s broad job: home, preparation or tension |
| **Close position** | Chord notes placed as near together as practical; useful for identifying the chord |
