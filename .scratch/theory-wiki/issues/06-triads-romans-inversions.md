# 06 — Triads, Roman numerals & inversions

**What to build:** The chord layer. A student in the triad-quality or inversion drills can read what a triad is (stacked thirds), how major/minor/diminished (and augmented, forward-linked to minor modes) qualities arise from interval stacking, how the seven diatonic triads fall out of a scale, what Roman-numeral notation means (case, symbols), and how figured bass 5/3–6–6/4 names inversions while the chord's identity survives a moving bass.

**Blocked by:** 04 — Intervals; 05 — Scales, keys & the circle of fifths.

**Status:** ready-for-agent

Scope — canonical pages in `wiki/chords/`: the triad (root/third/fifth, stacked thirds), triad qualities, diatonic triads of the major scale, Roman-numeral notation, chord inversion, figured bass for triads (5/3, 6, 6/4), doubling/spacing at the depth the Lab's inversion lab shows. Reference `cat:chords`, `cat:inversions`, the Lab inversions experiment, and the Atlas/graph surfaces that display chord nodes.
