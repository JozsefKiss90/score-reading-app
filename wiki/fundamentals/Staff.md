# Staff

## Definition

The staff is the grid music is written on: five horizontal lines and the four spaces between them. How high a note sits on the staff is its pitch; how far right it sits is when it happens.

## Why it matters

Every drill in this app shows you a score, and every score is drawn on staves. Reading the staff is the visual half of everything the Lab asks — the other half is your hands on the keyboard.

## Prerequisites

The [[Note Names]] — staff positions are those seven letters, stacked.

## Explanation

Each line and each space holds one letter name, in alphabetical order from bottom to top: a note on a line, then the space above it, then the next line, spell three consecutive letters. Move a note up one position and you have moved up one letter; after G the letters wrap to A and keep climbing.

*Which* letter belongs to which line is not fixed by the staff itself — that is the job of the [[Clef]] drawn at its left edge. Notes that outgrow the five lines continue on [[Ledger Line|ledger lines]] above or below.

Vertical bar lines slice the staff into measures — the time-keeping side of the grid, covered in [[Beat and Measure]].

## In the Lab

Every exercise renders its score on staves — in practice two of them joined into a [[Grand Staff]], one per hand. The drills never ask you to *write* on a staff, only to read one and answer at the keyboard.

## Common confusions

- **Line notes sit *on* the line, not above it.** The line passes through the note head. Space notes sit fully between lines. Mixing these up shifts every reading by one letter.
- **Height is pitch, not loudness or importance.** A note high on the staff is a higher note — nothing more.

## Related Concepts
