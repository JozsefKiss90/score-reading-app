# Beat and Measure

## Definition

The beat is music's steady underlying pulse — what you tap your foot to. A measure (or bar) is a small group of those beats, marked off on the staff by vertical bar lines.

## Why it matters

The drills play their scores back at a steady tempo and lay their material out measure by measure. You don't need deep rhythm theory here — the app asks only that "beat", "measure", and "bar line" mean something when a drill counts you in or a page says "one chord per measure".

## Prerequisites

The [[Staff]] — measures are how its left-to-right axis is divided.

## Explanation

Listen to almost any music and a pulse emerges: an evenly spaced tick you could clap along to. That tick is the **beat**. Beats naturally clump into repeating groups — most often four, sometimes three — and notation draws a **bar line** after each group, boxing it into a **measure**.

Counting restarts in every measure: "1 2 3 4 | 1 2 3 4 …". Beat 1, the **downbeat**, carries the most weight — harmonies in the drills tend to arrive on it. How many beats a measure holds, and which note value gets one beat, is declared by the [[Time Signature]].

One notational consequence you've already met: an [[Accidental]] lasts exactly to the end of its measure — the bar line is what cancels it.

## In the Lab

Drill playback runs on this grid: a steady tempo, harmonies placed on beats, scores unfolding a measure at a time on the [[Grand Staff]]. The technique drills lean hardest on it — their exercises subdivide every beat into fast, even notes, and playing them *in time with the count* is part of what's being practised.

## Common confusions

- **The beat is the grid, not the notes.** A measure can hold one long note or a flurry of short ones; the four beats underneath tick on unchanged either way.
- **"Bar" and "measure" are the same thing.** Two words, one concept — this vault uses *measure*, but "bar" and "bar line" appear everywhere in musical conversation.

## Related Concepts

The [[Time Signature]] declares each measure's size; the [[Staff]] carries the bar lines; the [[Grand Staff]] shows both hands sharing one and the same measure grid.
