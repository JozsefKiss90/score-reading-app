# Octave

## Definition

An octave is the distance from any note to the next higher (or lower) note with the same name — from one C to the neighbouring C. Notes an octave apart sound so alike that music treats them as versions of the same note.

## Why it matters

The octave is why seven letter names are enough for the whole keyboard, and why the app's grader accepts your answer in whatever register you play it. It is the fold line along which all of music's naming repeats.

## Prerequisites

[[Note Names]] and [[Keyboard Geography]] — the octave is the distance at which both repeat.

## Explanation

Play middle C, then the next C up: eight white keys if you count both ends (*octave* = eighth), and exactly twelve [[Semitone|semitones]]. The higher C vibrates twice as fast, which is why the two blend almost into one sound — the ear hears "the same note, higher".

To tell the many Cs apart, notation borrows a number: middle C is **C4**, the C above it C5, the C below C3. The number increments at each new C going up. You will meet these labels wherever an exact register matters (MIDI, and some app feedback).

On the [[Staff]], notes an octave apart sit seven positions apart — one on a line, the other in a space (bottom-line E up to top-space E in the treble clef). High and low octaves push notes onto [[Ledger Line|ledger lines]].

## In the Lab

The technique arpeggio runs climb chords octave by octave — the same three notes re-found in each register, which is octave fluency made physical. Elsewhere the octave works in your favour: the grader hears [[Pitch Class|pitch classes]], so a required chord counts in whichever octave your hands find comfortable.

## Common confusions

- **Same name ≠ same pitch.** C3 and C4 are the same pitch class but different pitches — an octave is a real distance, not zero.
- **The octave number changes at C, not at A.** B3 is followed by C4: the count rolls over at C, so A and B sit at the *top* of their octave number, which surprises many students.

## Related Concepts

[[Pitch Class]] is what remains of a note when you ignore its octave; [[Semitone]] is the small step twelve of which make an octave; [[Ledger Line|ledger lines]] carry the outer octaves beyond the staff.
