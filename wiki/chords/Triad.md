# Triad

## Definition

A triad is a three-note chord built by stacking two thirds: a root, a third above it, and a fifth above the root. It is the basic unit of harmony in this app.

## Why it matters

Almost everything the Lab drills — degrees, functions, cadences, inversions — is made of triads. Once you can spell and hear the four triad qualities, every later chord (sevenths, applied chords) is a triad plus one idea.

## Prerequisites

Know what a [[Third]] is and how the [[Major Scale]] names its notes.

## Explanation

Take C major. Start on C, skip a scale step, take E, skip again, take G: **C–E–G**. The distances between those notes decide the *quality*:

- **Major** — big third then small third (C–E–G). Bright, stable.
- **Minor** — small third then big third (A–C–E). Darker, still stable.
- **Diminished** — two small thirds (B–D–F). Tense, wants to resolve.
- **Augmented** — two big thirds (C–E–G♯). Unsettled; in this app it appears in harmonic-minor keys as III+.

Building a triad on each degree of a major scale yields the diatonic pattern the drills lean on constantly: major on 1, 4, 5; minor on 2, 3, 6; diminished on 7.

## In the Lab

The *Chords* category drills triad qualities directly: you are shown or played a triad and identify (or play) its quality across keys. Later lessons reuse the same triads under Roman numerals.

## In the Atlas

The Global Diatonic Map shows the seven diatonic triads of the reference key with their qualities and interval layers; the Quality Matrix regroups them by quality across all keys.

## Common confusions

- **Triad ≠ any three notes.** C–D–E is three notes but not a triad — triads are stacked in thirds.
- **Quality vs. degree.** "Minor triad" names an interval structure; "the ii chord" names a scale position. The ii chord in C major *happens* to be a minor triad — the two labels answer different questions.
