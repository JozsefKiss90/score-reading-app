# Triad

## Definition

A triad is a three-note chord built by stacking two thirds: a root, a third above it, and a fifth above the root. It is the basic unit of harmony in this app.

## Why it matters

Almost everything the Lab drills — degrees, functions, cadences, inversions — is made of triads. Once you can spell and hear the four triad qualities, every later chord (sevenths, applied chords) is a triad plus one idea.

## Prerequisites

Know what a [[Third]] is and how the [[Major Scale]] names its notes.

## Explanation

Take C major. Start on C, skip a scale step, take E, skip again, take G: **C–E–G**. The three members have names that stay attached no matter how the chord is later rearranged:

- **Root** — the note the triad is built from and named after (C).
- **Third** — a [[Third]] above the root (E).
- **Fifth** — a [[Perfect Fifth]] above the root (G), in the common qualities.

Two ideas branch off this construction, each with its own page. The *sizes* of the two stacked thirds decide whether the triad is major, minor, diminished or augmented — [[Triad Quality]] takes that apart interval by interval. And because "skip a step" can start on any scale note, a scale yields a whole chord family at once: [[Diatonic Triads]] lays out the seven every key contains.

## In the Lab

The *Chords* category drills triad qualities directly: you are shown or played a triad and identify (or play) its quality across keys. Later lessons reuse the same triads under Roman numerals.

## In the Atlas

The Global Diatonic Map shows the seven diatonic triads of the reference key with their qualities and interval layers; the Quality Matrix regroups them by quality across all keys.

## Common confusions

- **Triad ≠ any three notes.** C–D–E is three notes but not a triad — triads are stacked in thirds.
- **Quality vs. degree.** "Minor triad" names an interval structure; "the ii chord" names a scale position — two labels answering different questions. The *Triad Quality* page pulls them apart properly.
