# Roman Numeral

## Definition

Roman-numeral notation names a chord by the [[Scale Degree]] its root stands on — I through VII — with letter case and symbols encoding the [[Triad Quality]]. It says what a chord *is in its key*, not what its notes are called.

## Why it matters

Numerals are the app's chord language because they are key-proof. "G major" is a fact about one key's dominant; "V" is a fact about *every* key's dominant. A progression learned as I–IV–V–I transfers to all twelve keys unchanged, and the drills exploit exactly that: the spelling changes every round while the numeral stays put. Everything downstream — [[Harmonic Function|functions]], cadences, [[Chord Inversion|inversions]], seventh chords — is written in this notation.

## Prerequisites

[[Scale Degree]] numbering (the numeral *is* a degree number), [[Diatonic Triads]] (the chords being named), and [[Triad Quality]] (what the case encodes).

## Explanation

**The number is the root's degree.** V means "the triad built on degree 5̂". In C major that is G–B–D; in G major, D–F♯–A. Same numeral, same role, different notes.

**The case and symbols are the quality**, exactly as the drills render them:

- **Uppercase = major**: I, IV, V in a major key.
- **Lowercase = minor**: ii, iii, vi.
- **° = diminished**: vii° — lowercase, because the third above the root is minor.
- **+ = augmented**: III+ — uppercase, because the third is major; met in harmonic-minor keys.

So the major key reads I–ii–iii–IV–V–vi–vii°, and natural minor reads i–ii°–III–iv–v–VI–VII. A numeral in a minor key is counted against the *minor* scale: VII in A minor is the chord on G, a whole tone below home.

**Two names for every chord.** The drills usually show a numeral and a chord symbol side by side — "V" and "G". The chord symbol is the absolute name (which notes); the numeral is the relative one (which job, in which key). Both are true at once, and translating between them fluently is much of what the degree drills train.

**Numerals are meaningless without a key.** "V" answers the question "the fifth chord *of what?*". C major's triad is I in C major, IV in G major, V in F major — one sound, three numerals, depending on who is asking.

**Digits after the numeral** — ii6, I6/4, V65 — are not new chords but bass instructions, the subject of [[Figured Bass]].

## In the Lab

The *Degrees & Transposition* category makes the notation itself the lesson: each drill fixes one numeral — I through vii° in major, i through VII in minor — and transposes its chord through all twelve keys, block or arpeggiated. The point of the exercise is the numeral's invariance: name the key and the numeral aloud each round and notice which one changed.

## In the Atlas

The Global Diatonic Map heads each of its seven columns with the degree's numeral, quality-cased exactly as above.

## Common confusions

- **Case is content.** IV and iv are different chords (F major vs. F minor in C). Reading numerals case-blind discards half the symbol.
- **Numeral ≠ degree.** 5̂ names one note; V names the three-note chord standing on it. The caret and the case keep the two systems apart.
- **"vii° is lowercase because it's the seventh chord"** — no: it is lowercase because its quality has a minor third, and marked ° because the fifth is diminished. Every part of the symbol is quality information, none of it is position information except the number itself.
- **Minor-key numerals count from the minor tonic.** III in A minor is C major — degree 3̂ of *A minor*, not anything to do with C major's own numbering.
