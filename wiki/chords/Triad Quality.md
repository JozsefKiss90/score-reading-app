# Triad Quality

## Definition

A triad's quality — major, minor, diminished or augmented — is decided by the sizes of the two thirds it stacks. Quality is what a triad sounds like from the inside, regardless of which key it appears in or which note it is built on.

## Why it matters

Quality is the key-proof half of chord knowledge. A minor triad sounds minor whether it is the ii chord of C major or the tonic of F♯ minor, so an ear (or eye) trained on the four qualities transfers everywhere. The app leans on this constantly: quality decides a numeral's letter case, distinguishes the dominant chords that must resolve from the tonic chords that may rest, and later extends into the qualities of four-note chords like the [[Dominant Seventh Chord]].

## Prerequisites

The [[Triad]] and its root–third–fifth members, and the [[Third]] with its two sizes — major (4 semitones) and minor (3).

## Explanation

Stack two thirds and there are exactly four size combinations, bottom up:

| Quality | Stacked thirds | Outer fifth | Example | Character |
|---------|----------------|-------------|---------|-----------|
| Major | major + minor | perfect | C–E–G | bright, stable |
| Minor | minor + major | perfect | A–C–E | dark, stable |
| Diminished | minor + minor | diminished | B–D–F | tense, must move |
| Augmented | major + major | augmented | C–E–G♯ | suspended, directionless |

**The fifth is the frame.** Major and minor share a [[Perfect Fifth]] between root and fifth; only the middle note moves, a semitone either way. Diminished shrinks the frame to a [[Tritone]] — that, not the lowered third, is what makes it unstable. Augmented stretches the frame a semitone past perfect and, being two identical thirds, has no audible bottom: every note could be the root.

**Stability follows the intervals.** [[Consonance and Dissonance]] explains the mechanism: the two qualities with a perfect fifth are consonant and can end a phrase; the two with an altered fifth are dissonant and demand resolution.

**Where each quality lives.** A major key contains three of the four for free — [[Diatonic Triads]] shows which degrees carry which. The augmented triad is diatonic to *no* major key: in this app it appears as III+ in [[Harmonic Minor Scale]] keys, where the raised seventh degree stretches the third chord's fifth.

## In the Lab

The *Chords (Triads)* category's *Triad qualities* lesson sorts drills by quality: you play every major triad across the keys, then every minor one, then the diminished set, hearing the same colour survive changing notes. The augmented drill draws from harmonic minor, where III+ is native. Practice cue the drills give: spell the stacked thirds — M3+m3, m3+M3, m3+m3, where M3 is a major third and m3 a minor one — rather than reading the key signature.

## Common confusions

- **Minor vs. diminished.** Both start with a minor third; the fifth is the tell. Perfect fifth: minor. Tritone: diminished.
- **Reading quality off the key signature.** Quality lives in the triad's own intervals. B–D–F is diminished in C major, in F major, and on a desert island.
- **Quality vs. position.** "Minor triad" describes interval structure; "the ii chord" describes where it sits in a key. The [[Roman Numeral]] notation fuses both into one symbol, which is convenient but worth keeping mentally separate.
- **Augmented is rare, not exotic.** It is simply the one combination the major scale can't produce; harmonic minor produces it in exactly one place.
