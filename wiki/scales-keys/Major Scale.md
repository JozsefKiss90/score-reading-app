# Major Scale

## Definition

The major scale is a seven-note pattern of whole and half steps — **W W H W W W H** — starting from its home note, so C major runs C D E F G A B. Every major key is this one pattern started from a different note.

## Why it matters

The major scale is the note-pool the drills draw from: it decides which seven notes belong in a key and which chords those notes can build. Learn the pattern once and you have not learned "C major" — you have learned all twelve major keys, because they are the same shape laid onto different starting notes. That single idea is why the app can transpose nearly every drill.

## Prerequisites

The [[Note Names]] loop, the [[Semitone]] and the [[Whole Tone]] as the two step sizes, and the [[Accidental]] for spelling the pattern away from C.

## Explanation

**Build it from C.** Start on C and walk the pattern: whole (C–D), whole (D–E), half (E–F), whole (F–G), whole (G–A), whole (A–B), half (B–C). It lands back on C an [[Octave]] up, having used every white key exactly once — the half steps fall naturally at E–F and B–C, the keyboard's two gaps without a black key.

**One letter each.** A scale uses each of the seven letters exactly once. That rule is what makes the spelling of every other major scale automatic: start on G and the pattern runs G A B C D E F♯ — the seventh step must be some kind of F, and the pattern demands the raised one. Start on F and you need B♭. Each new starting note forces its own set of sharps or flats, which is why a [[Key]] can be spelled once at the start of the line as a [[Key Signature]] instead of marking accidentals note by note.

**Positions have names.** Within the scale, each note is a numbered position — first, second, third — called a [[Scale Degree]], and the degrees carry names (tonic, dominant, and so on) that stay meaningful in every key. The seventh degree, a semitone below the tonic, is the [[Leading Tone]] — the note that gives the scale its pull home.

**The pattern generates the harmony.** Build a [[Triad]] on each degree and the qualities come out in a fixed order — major, minor, minor, major, major, minor, diminished — identical in every major key. The scale is not just a melody-ladder; it is the source of the key's whole **diatonic** chord set (*diatonic* = built only from the scale's own seven notes), laid out in full at [[Diatonic Triads]] and exercised by the drills.

Twelve starting notes give twelve major keys, and the [[Circle of Fifths]] arranges them by how many accidentals the pattern forces.

## Common confusions

- **"Major scale = white keys."** Only C major is all white keys. The scale is the *pattern* W W H W W W H; on any other starting note it needs black keys to keep the step sizes right.
- **The half steps are invisible in the letters.** C–D and E–F look like equal moves on paper, but one is a whole tone and the other a semitone. The pattern lives on the keyboard, not in the alphabet.
- **Scale ≠ key.** The scale is the ordered inventory of notes; the key is the music's allegiance to that inventory and its home note. You play a scale; you are *in* a key.
