# Circle of Fifths

## Definition

The circle of fifths arranges the twelve keys so that each step clockwise moves up a perfect fifth and adds one sharp (or removes one flat). Neighbouring keys on the circle share all but one note.

## Why it matters

The circle is the map of key relationships: it tells you which keys are close (easy to move between, common in real pieces) and which are far. The app transposes almost every drill around the circle, so recognising the pattern turns twelve separate keys into one repeating idea.

## Prerequisites

Know the [[Major Scale]] and what a key signature is. *(Key-signature and relative-minor pages arrive with ticket 05.)*

## Explanation

Start at C major — no sharps, no flats. Go clockwise: G major gains F♯; D major adds C♯; each new sharp is a fifth above the last. Go counter-clockwise from C instead: F major gains B♭, then B♭ major adds E♭, and so on through the flat keys. Twelve steps either way returns home.

Two practical readings of the circle:

- **Distance = relatedness.** C and G differ by one accidental; C and F♯ differ by six. A chord progression or modulation between neighbours sounds smooth because the scales overlap almost entirely.
- **Position = key signature.** The number of steps from C *is* the number of sharps or flats — no memorising twelve signatures separately.

The same wheel carries the relative minors on its inner ring (A minor inside C major), since a relative pair shares one signature.

The Circle overview in the curriculum's *Circle of Fifths* category walks the wheel key by key; the *Scales* category then drills the scales the circle organises, block and arpeggiated, around the same clockwise order — the drills' key sequence is the circle made audible. Triads built on each key's degrees keep their qualities as you travel, which is why the drills transpose so freely (see [[Triads|triad]]).

## Common confusions

- **Clockwise ≠ "higher".** Moving C → G goes up a fifth *or* down a fourth; the circle tracks key identity, not register.
- **Circle of fifths vs. scale of fifths.** The circle orders *keys*; it is not a scale you play. Playing twelve fifths in a row spans seven octaves — the circle folds that spiral into a wheel by treating octave-equivalent notes as the same.
