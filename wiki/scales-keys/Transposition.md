# Transposition

## Definition

Transposition moves music to a different key: every note shifts by the same [[Interval]], so all the internal relationships — steps, chords, contour — survive unchanged. A tune transposed from C major to E♭ major has new pitches and the same shape.

## Why it matters

Transposition is the app's core pedagogical bet: learn a shape once *as degrees*, and you own it in twelve keys. The degree drills, the motive drills, and the key-by-key sweep of nearly every drill family are all transposition in action. It is also the test of real understanding — a pattern you can only play in C was memorised as letters, not learned as music.

## Prerequisites

The [[Key]] and [[Major Scale]], the [[Scale Degree]] vocabulary (transposition is degree-thinking applied), and the [[Interval]] as the unit everything shifts by.

## Explanation

**Think in degrees, not letters.** The motive 1̂–3̂–5̂–3̂ is C–E–G–E in C major and E♭–G–B♭–G in E♭ major. As letters, those are two things to learn; as degrees, one. Transposing means re-anchoring the tonic and letting the degrees re-spell themselves through the new key's [[Key Signature]].

**What survives, what changes.** Preserved: every interval, every chord quality, the degree pattern, the contour — everything a listener recognises as *the same music*. Changed: the letters, the accidentals, the signature, the hand position, and the absolute height. A [[Triad]] on degree 5̂ is major in every major key; only its spelling moves.

**Distance between keys.** The [[Circle of Fifths]] is the transposer's map: neighbouring keys differ by one accidental, so transposing one clockwise step re-spells exactly one note. The drills sweep keys in circle order for precisely this reason — each new key is a one-note edit of the last.

**Octave shifts are the trivial case.** Moving music up or down a whole [[Octave]] is technically a transposition, but it lands on the same letter names in the same key — same music, different register. "Transposition" in the app always means the interesting case: a change of key.

## In the Lab

Two drill families make transposition itself the skill. The *Degrees & Transposition* category fixes one degree and carries its triad through all twelve keys — the same numeral, twelve spellings. The *Motives* category's *Motive transposition* lesson does the melodic version: one degree pattern (like 1̂–3̂–5̂–3̂) played around the circle of fifths, where the cell's identity — the [[Motive]] — is exactly what transposition preserves. Beyond those, almost every drill family exists in all keys, so the whole curriculum quietly repeats the lesson.

## In the Atlas

The Transposition Matrix (Atlas Part II) is the concept as a table: every degree of every key side by side, one column per circle step — read across a row to watch one degree transpose.

## Common confusions

- **Transposition vs. octave shift.** Playing the same passage an octave higher keeps the key; transposition proper changes it. Only the second forces new spellings.
- **"The notes changed, so it's different music."** Everything a listener tracks — intervals, qualities, contour — is intact. Recognising sameness across keys is the skill, and it is trainable.
- **Transposition vs. modulation.** Transposing restates a *whole* piece or pattern in a new key; modulation is a piece changing key *mid-stream* as part of its own story. The chromaticism pages take up modulation later.
