# SUMMARY TABLE

## Third-based chords – triads and sevenths

> **Reading key:**
>
> * the **3rd degree** decides: major vs. minor
> * the **5th degree** modifies stability
> * the **7th degree** introduces temporal tension

---

## I. THIRD-BASED TRIADS

| Chord type     | Scale degree formula | Interval layering (from root) | Fifth type       | Semantic character            |
| -------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Major**      | 1–3–5                | M3 + m3                       | perfect (P5)     | stable, open                  |
| **Minor**      | 1–♭3–5               | m3 + M3                       | perfect (P5)     | stable, inward-turning        |
| **Diminished** | 1–♭3–♭5              | m3 + m3                       | diminished (d5)  | unstable, demands resolution  |
| **Augmented**  | 1–3–♯5               | M3 + M3                       | augmented (A5)   | floating, directionless       |

### Key observation (triads):

> **The major/minor quality is determined solely by the lower third (1–3).**
> The fifth **does not decide**, only modifies.

---

## II. THIRD-BASED SEVENTH CHORDS

> The seventh chord = **triad + seventh**
> The seventh is **not color**, but **temporal direction**.

---

### 1. Stable foundation + floating upper tension

| Chord type      | Scale degree formula | Interval layering | Seventh  | Semantics                      |
| --------------- | -------------------- | ----------------- | -------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Major 7th**   | 1–3–5–7              | M3 + m3 + M3      | major 7  | stable foundation, open ending |
| **Minor-major7**| 1–♭3–5–7             | m3 + M3 + M3      | major 7  | internal tension, floating     |

---

### 2. Directed (resolution-implying) structures

| Chord type    | Scale degree formula | Interval layering | Seventh | Semantics                       |
| ------------- | -------------------- | ----------------- | ------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Dominant7** | 1–3–5–♭7             | M3 + m3 + m3      | minor 7 | strong resolution compulsion    |
| **Minor7**    | 1–♭3–5–♭7            | m3 + M3 + m3      | minor 7 | softer, cyclical tension        |

---

### 3. Unstable-foundation sevenths

| Chord type               | Scale degree formula | Interval layering | Fifth      | Seventh    | Semantics            |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- | ----------------- | ---------- | ---------- | -------------------- |
| **Half-diminished (m7♭5)** | 1–♭3–♭5–♭7          | m3 + m3 + M3      | diminished | minor 7    | transitional, seeking |
| **Fully diminished 7**   | 1–♭3–♭5–♭♭7          | m3 + m3 + m3      | diminished | diminished | maximum tension      |

---

## III. QUICK DECISION TREE (MENTAL LEXICON)

**When you look at a chord:**

1. **What is the 3rd degree?**

   * 3 → major
   * ♭3 → minor

2. **What is the 5th degree?**

   * 5 → stable
   * ♭5 / ♯5 → unstable or floating

3. **Is there a 7th degree?**

   * none → state (triad)
   * ♭7 → directed tension
   * 7 → floating tension
   * ♭♭7 → symmetric, multi-directional tension

The **name is then just a label.**
